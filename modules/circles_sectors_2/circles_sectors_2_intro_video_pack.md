# Circles and Circle Sectors (2) Intro Video Pack

Mission: Lesson 26 - Circles and Circle Sectors (2)

## Learning Outcome

By the end of this intro video the learner can find awkward shaded areas around circles by two moves: splitting and combining figures so the pieces are easy shapes, and taking the blank area away from the whole. They can subtract an inscribed circle from its square (corners = (2r)² − πr²), take a quarter circle away from a square and leave the answer in terms of π (s² − ¼πs²), find the central gap between four packed circles by joining the centres ((2r)² − πr²), work out overlapping-semicircle petals (2πr² − 4r²) and the plain corners around them, find the leaf-shaped lens where two quarter circles overlap ((½π − 1)s²), and slice a right isosceles triangle out of a quarter circle (¼πr² − ½r²), using whichever value of π the question gives (3.14, 22/7, 3, or leaving π in the answer).

## Named Skills Shown Before Practice

- Circle in a square
- Quarter circle shade
- Four circle gap
- Petal power
- Leaf lens
- Flag slice

Each named skill below appears as one storyboard scene, one intro-scene title in the module JS, and one entry in the registry skills array, so the vocabulary stays identical everywhere the child meets it.

## Storyboard

1. **Circle in a square**
   Purpose: subtract the inscribed circle from the square to get the corners.
   Voiceover: When a circle fits exactly inside a square, the side of the square equals the circle's diameter, two r. So the whole square is two r times two r, and the blank circle inside is pi r squared. Take the circle away and the four shaded corners remain. With radius ten and pi as three point one four, that is four hundred minus three hundred and fourteen, which is eighty six.

2. **Quarter circle shade**
   Purpose: take a quarter circle away from a square, keeping the answer in terms of π.
   Voiceover: A quarter circle with the same radius as the square's side sweeps from one corner. The shaded leftover is the square minus the quarter circle, which is s squared minus a quarter of pi s squared. For a side of twenty, that is four hundred minus one hundred pi, and you can leave the answer written with pi in it when the question asks.

3. **Four circle gap**
   Purpose: find the middle gap between four packed circles by joining the centres.
   Voiceover: Four identical circles fill a big square, so the big side is four radiuses long. Now draw a small square joining the four centres. Its side is two r, and inside it each circle contributes a quarter, which together make one whole circle. So the middle gap is the small square minus one circle, two r squared minus pi r squared.

4. **Petal power**
   Purpose: overlapping semicircles make petals — circles minus the square.
   Voiceover: Draw a semicircle on each edge of a square and they overlap in four petal shapes. All four semicircles together make two pi r squared. They blanket the whole square once, and the petals get covered a second time. So the extra layer is the petals, two pi r squared minus four r squared, and the plain corners are the square minus those petals.

5. **Leaf lens**
   Purpose: two quarter circles overlap in a lens — add the quarters, subtract the square.
   Voiceover: Sweep a quarter circle from one corner of the square and another from the opposite corner. Together they cover every point of the square at least once and the leaf shaped lens in the middle exactly twice. So add the two quarter circles and subtract the square, leaving half pi minus one, times s squared. With side ten that leaf is fifty seven.

6. **Flag slice**
   Purpose: quarter circle minus the right isosceles triangle inside it.
   Voiceover: Inside a quarter circle, draw the triangle joining the two straight edges. It is right angled and isosceles with legs r, so its area is half r squared. The quarter circle is a quarter pi r squared. Take the triangle away and the curved slice that remains is a quarter pi r squared minus half r squared. With radius eight and pi as three, that is sixteen.

## Warmup Gate

Before full practice, the learner should answer:

1. A circle of radius 10 fits exactly inside a square. Why is the square's side 20, and what is the corner area with π = 3.14?
2. A quarter circle of radius 20 sits in a square of side 20. Why is the shaded leftover 400 − 100π rather than 400 − 400π?
3. Two quarter circles from opposite corners cover the square once and the lens twice. Why does quarter + quarter − square give exactly the lens?

## Misconception Risks

These are the common pitfalls children bring to circle and sector areas:

- Using the side as the radius when it is really the diameter, like Pip in the lesson who halved the wrong number for the rectangle along the circle's diameter.
- Subtracting in the wrong order (circle minus square) or forgetting that a quarter circle is ¼πr², not πr² ÷ 2.
- Treating overlap as if it were covered once: petals and lenses are covered twice by the overlapping shapes, which is exactly why circles − square isolates them.
- Mixing π values mid-problem — each question fixes π as 3.14, 22/7, 3, or asks for the answer in terms of π; the chosen value must be used everywhere in that question.

## Voiceover Draft

This is the full voiceover draft. Each paragraph maps to one storyboard scene and is written to be read aloud to an eleven or twelve year old, naming the skill and pointing at what changes on screen during that beat of the intro.

Circle in a square. The circle swells until it kisses all four sides, showing the side equals the diameter, two r. The square glows as the whole, the circle fades as the blank, and the four corners stay shaded: two r squared minus pi r squared. Radius ten, pi three point one four — four hundred minus three hundred and fourteen leaves eighty six.

Quarter circle shade. One corner anchors a quarter circle that sweeps across the square, radius equal to the side. What escapes the sweep is the shade: s squared minus a quarter pi s squared. With side twenty, the screen writes four hundred minus one hundred pi — and the pi stays in the answer, exactly as asked.

Four circle gap. Four circles roll into the big square, two across and two down, so the big side is four r. A small orange square snaps between the four centres, side two r. Each circle donates a quarter into it — one whole circle — so the middle gap is the small square minus one circle.

Petal power. Semicircles bloom from each edge and overlap into four petals. Watch the count: the four semicircles paint the square once, and paint the petals a second time. So two pi r squared minus four r squared is exactly the petal layer, and the untouched corners are the square minus the petals.

Leaf lens. A quarter circle sweeps from one corner, a second from the opposite corner, and a leaf appears where they cross. Every point of the square is covered once, the leaf twice. Add the two quarters, subtract the square, and the leaf is half pi minus one, times s squared — fifty seven when the side is ten.

Flag slice. The triangle stretches between the quarter circle's two straight edges, right angled and isosceles with legs r. The quarter is a quarter pi r squared; the triangle is half r squared. Slide the triangle away and the curved slice glows: with radius eight and pi three, forty eight minus thirty two leaves sixteen.
